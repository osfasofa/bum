You showed up. The seat was already here. A browser tab owns no hardware — it asks, and the OS decides what to hand over. This page asks for everything, one thing at a time, and shows you exactly what came back: what you can bring in, what you can send out, and what this particular seat will let you do with it.

Everything here runs in your tab. No network calls, no analytics, no upload — the Content-Security-Policy on this site sets connect-src 'none', so the browser enforces that rather than asking you to take our word for it. Saved profiles live in this browser's localStorage; exports download to your own disk. Nothing about your gear leaves this machine.

0 · the seat

The machine you sat down at, and how much of it this page is allowed to see at all.

None of this needs permission. It is the browser describing its own situation.

1 · permissions

The gate. Nothing named, nothing captured, until you say yes here.

State comes from the Permissions API where the browser has one, and from what actually happened where it doesn't.

2 · allowed

What this browser, on this machine, at this URL, will let a page do at all. No permission needed to find out.

Half of these are decided by the address bar, not by you: https or localhost, or most of it is off.

3 · engine

The AudioContext is the rack. Nothing sounds until it is running, and a browser will not start one without a click.

The sample rate is normally chosen for you by the current default output device. You can try to insist.

4 · ins

Every audio input the OS is willing to mention. Arm one to find out what it really is: how many channels, at what rate, with which processing forced on.

Arm as many as you like. Each is its own stream with its own clock drift.

5 · mixer

Every armed input gets a strip: level, placement, mute, solo. This is the part that makes the tab an interface rather than a readout.

Two outputs and you get a pan control. More than two and you get to choose which physical socket each input lands on.

6 · outs

One engine speaks to one device. If that device has eight outs you address them as channels, discretely, one at a time.

A second device means a second engine and a second clock. There is no way around that from a tab.

7 · midi

Controllers and synths are gear too. Separate permission, separate API, no audio engine required.

Inputs show live bytes as they arrive. Outputs will take a note from you.

8 · screens

The display is an output, and usually the only one anybody notices. Here is what it can actually show: gamut, dynamic range, refresh.

9 · cameras

Not the point of this page, but they are ins, and an honest inventory counts them.

10 · human input

You, as a device. A mouse reports a position. A stylus reports pressure, tilt and twist, and most pages throw all of it away.

Draw on the pad and watch what your hardware actually sends.

11 · wired & wireless

Everything that plugs in and refuses to be MIDI. Control surfaces, microcontrollers, whatever you built.

A page never gets a list of what is connected. It gets the one thing you hand it, and nothing else.

12 · sensors

The machine noticing the room it is in. Tilt, spin, magnetic north, how bright it is, roughly where you are.

Mostly a phone story. On a desktop this section is honest about having nothing.

13 · other outs

The rest of what a page can push into the world: a voice, a buzz, a screen kept awake, a notification, the clipboard.

14 · profile

What you had armed, pointed at, and mixed — kept in this browser so the seat remembers you.

The export is every section above as JSON, on your disk, for you.

log

What happened, and why the browser behaves that way.
